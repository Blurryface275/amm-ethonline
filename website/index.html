Adaptive Volatility-Weighted AMM
with a Dynamic Fee Mechanism

A Uniswap v4 hook that prices every swap by a volatility-tiered dynamic LP fee — and spikes the fee the moment it detects a same-block MEV move. Volatility is computed off-chain from a subgraph and bridged on-chain via Chainlink Functions. Fail-safe by design: stale data never drops fees open.

The problem

Static AMM fees bleed value exactly when it matters most.

🔁 Adverse selection in volatility

When markets move fast, informed traders swap against LPs at stale prices. A fixed fee doesn't compensate LPs for the extra risk, so they lose money precisely when volatility spikes.

🥪 Sandwiches & LVR

Same-block MEV (sandwich attacks, back-running) extracts value from ordinary swappers. A fee that can't react within the block leaves that value on the table.

🧊 One-size-fits-all fees

A calm pool and a chaotic pool pay the same fee. Calm pools over-charge and lose volume; volatile pools under-charge and subsidize toxic flow.

Our solution

A hook that makes the fee a function of risk — and reacts inside the block.

① Volatility-tiered dynamic fee

Each pool's recent realized volatility maps to a fee tier: low (calm) → medium → high (volatile). The hook returns the tier fee from beforeSwap using Uniswap v4's dynamic-fee flag. LPs get paid for risk; calm pools stay competitive.

② Same-block MEV spike

Independent of the tier, the hook records the block's opening price and compares every later swap against it. A move past the threshold overrides the tier with a flat spike fee — taxing sandwich/back-run flow the moment it appears.

③ Fail-safe, not fail-open

Missing or stale volatility never drops fees — it falls back to the high tier and never reverts the swap. No governance surface on the fee parameters: a governable fee is itself an attack vector.

④ Off-chain compute, on-chain trust

A subgraph records raw swap history. Chainlink Functions computes realized volatility off-chain (so the formula can change without a resync) and writes it on-chain through the hook's keeper. The hook treats the keeper as opaque.

How it works

Primary path: subgraph → Chainlink Functions → hook → PoolManager. A fallback on-chain TWAP estimator exists if the bridge is unavailable.

The hook is the only contract traders touch. The keeper (VolatilityConsumer) is bound exactly once at deploy and cannot be changed — a one-time address bind, not a governable parameter.
